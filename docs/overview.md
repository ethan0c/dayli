Dayli — Your life, already organized.
Product concept & feature summary · October 2026
My favorite name is Dayli. It’s short, friendly, memorable, and doesn’t restrict the product to students, calendars, or productivity. It feels like an app someone would use every day.
Other names worth considering are Tendi, Orbi, Sundays, Pace, and Kivo. These are creative suggestions, not verified available trademarks or domains.
1. Product overview
Dayli is an AI-powered personal life organizer that helps busy people manage their schedules, responsibilities, deadlines, and daily routines without the tedious work of maintaining a planner.
Unlike traditional productivity apps, which require users to manually create tasks, enter calendar events, and organize information, Dayli allows users to simply send information they already have.
Users can text the app, upload screenshots, share documents, import syllabi, or connect supported calendars. Dayli interprets the information, organizes it, identifies conflicts, and helps users plan their time.
The central idea: You shouldn’t have to organize your life to use an app that’s supposed to organize it for you.
2. Who we’re building for
Our initial target audience is college students balancing school, part-time jobs, clinical rotations, and personal responsibilities.
The inspiration is a final-year nursing student who works at Olive Garden, receives her work schedule only a few days before the upcoming week, and has to juggle assignments, exams, classes, and work.
However, Dayli will be designed as a general-purpose product that can eventually serve working professionals, shift workers, parents, and anyone with a busy or unpredictable schedule.
We’ll launch with working college students because their scheduling problems are particularly clear, but the underlying system will support a broader audience.
3. Core product features
A. Effortless information capture
This is Dayli’s most important differentiator.
Text-to-add
Users can send a natural-language message inside Dayli or via SMS.
Example: “I work Friday 4–10, have an exam Monday, and need to buy scrubs Wednesday.”
Dayli identifies events, tasks, deadlines, and reminders, asks about missing information when necessary, and saves the confirmed items.
Screenshot-to-add
Users upload or share screenshots of work schedules, school portals, messages, and other important information.
AI extracts the relevant dates, times, and responsibilities. Users review the results and confirm them with one tap.
Document-to-add
Users upload PDFs, syllabi, course schedules, or other documents.
Dayli extracts important information, including class times, assignment deadlines, exam dates, and recurring commitments.
Voice-to-add
Users speak naturally to add events, reminders, and tasks without typing.
All four methods use a shared AI extraction system and structured calendar/task database.
B. Smart work schedule management
Dayli will be particularly useful for people with unpredictable work schedules.
The app will support importing schedules from screenshots, documents, calendar feeds, and authorized scheduling-system integrations where available.
When a new schedule arrives, Dayli compares it with the existing one to identify new, modified, removed, and unchanged shifts.
Instead of duplicating events, Dayli shows users what changed and asks them to confirm.
If next week’s schedule hasn’t been published, Dayli displays it as pending rather than assuming the user is available.
C. School and course management
Students will have a dedicated school area containing their courses, assignments, exams, and class schedules.
Each course can include its name, professor, meeting times, syllabus, assignments, exam dates, and grading information.
The standout feature is syllabus importing. Students upload their syllabi once, and Dayli automatically creates course records, extracts deadlines, and organizes the semester.
Students can also import screenshots from course registration portals to establish their initial class schedules, then add syllabi for more detailed information.
A full Canvas integration and GPA calculator are not required for the MVP. They may be introduced later, with Canvas offering particular value for keeping assignment dates synchronized when professors make changes.
D. Unified calendar and daily dashboard
Dayli combines school, work, and personal responsibilities into one calendar.
The home screen focuses on what matters immediately: today’s schedule, upcoming deadlines, pending tasks, and important changes.
Users can switch between daily, weekly, and monthly views, but the experience should remain simple and uncluttered.
Dayli should make it easy to understand what’s happening without forcing users to browse multiple screens.
E. Intelligent planning and conflict detection
Dayli doesn’t simply store events. It helps users respond to them.
When a work shift conflicts with a study session or a busy week threatens an upcoming deadline, Dayli can recommend adjustments.
For example, if a student receives two weekend shifts before a Monday exam, Dayli might suggest moving study sessions earlier, splitting exam preparation into smaller blocks, and preserving rest time.
Fixed commitments won’t be moved automatically. Users approve suggested changes.
F. Reminders and proactive assistance
Dayli will provide contextual notifications for upcoming events, deadlines, and schedule changes.
Rather than bombarding users with notifications, reminders should be configurable and focused on actionable information.
A future version could generate a short daily briefing, summarizing the day’s commitments and the most important upcoming responsibilities.

5. Development roadmap
Phase	Focus	Features
MVP	Capture and organize	Text-to-add, SMS-to-add, screenshot/PDF importing, course management, calendar, tasks, conflict detection
V1.1	Intelligent planning	Study suggestions, schedule replanning, smarter reminders, Google Calendar sync
V2	Deeper automation	Canvas integration, supported work schedule sync, shared calendars, grade tracking, proactive updates
For the MVP, accuracy and simplicity matter more than feature count. We should retain original import sources, flag ambiguous dates, prevent duplicate events, and let users undo changes.
6. Business model
I’d initially make Dayli free while testing the product with a small group of students.
Once the experience is reliable, a freemium model could make sense:
Free	Dayli Plus
Calendar and task management	Higher AI import limits
Basic text-to-add	More document and syllabus imports
Limited screenshot imports	Advanced schedule planning
Course management	SMS assistant and enhanced integrations
SMS and AI document processing have ongoing costs, so unlimited usage shouldn’t be promised without understanding the economics.
7. What makes Dayli different
Dayli’s competitors aren’t just calendars. They’re also task managers, student planners, and AI productivity assistants.
The advantage we’re trying to build is minimal input effort.
People shouldn’t have to spend time organizing information before a productivity app becomes useful.
We want the experience to feel like sending information to a reliable personal assistant that remembers it, organizes it, and brings it back when relevant.
The product’s success should be measured by how quickly users can import their responsibilities, how accurately Dayli understands them, and whether users keep trusting it week after week.

I’d lean toward the warm lavender direction: clean typography, subtle colors, friendly language, and minimal visual clutter. It should feel like a calm personal assistant rather than a corporate productivity tool.
Recommended name: Dayli Tagline: Your life, already organized. Positioning: The personal assistant that turns scattered information into an organized life. Initial audience: Busy college students, especially those balancing work and school. Long-term vision: A personal operating system that manages the logistics of everyday life.
Before committing to Dayli, we’d need to check trademark, App Store, and domain availability. The name is appealing, but it’s also close to the common word “daily,” so distinctiveness and searchability need validation.
The most important principle I’d carry into development is simple: Dayli should never become another responsibility its users have to manage.
